# The 8409 Bar

Build book: freestanding lounge bar for the basement game room (blank wall at the foot of the stairs, facing the pool table) + upstairs wet bar alcove refresh.
Status: concept, pending measurements. Date: 2026-11-08.
Drawings (plan, front elevation, section, back bar, alcove before/after) are in `the-8409-bar.pdf` and the HTML page in this folder.

## Reading the house

Every finished room runs the same formula: warm white shell, one heavy black element, one piece of warm organic wood, one texture that carries the room (brick, leather, geometric wallpaper). The dining table states it most clearly: live-edge acacia split by a black river on black steel. The media console repeats it in reverse: black box, round brass pulls. The powder room shows the dark mode: near-black walls, fine geometric line, one warm light.

The bar should be the dining table's sibling, not a new idea. Black slatted body, acacia top, black pipe foot rail, brass only at pulls and shelf brackets. The wall behind it goes the powder-room route: deep espresso-black, matte.

Palette
- Wall white: the shell
- Console black: bar body, cabinet paint
- Acacia: bar top, alcove top, shelves
- Cognac: sectional, stool seats
- Aged brass: pulls and brackets only
- Brick: fireplace, untouched
- Espresso: back-bar wall, alcove niche

## The position

Build one showpiece bar, downstairs, in front of the blank wall at the foot of the stairs where it overlooks the pool table. Turn the upstairs alcove into the service station (sink, ice, glass storage) rather than a second showpiece. Two display bars on two floors doubles the spend on the parts that cost money: top and hardware.

Counterargument, which is real: the alcove already has cabinetry and probably plumbing, so the cheapest "sick bar" is upgrading the alcove alone for roughly a quarter of this budget. If money is the binding constraint this month, do that and stop. I still recommend the basement bar because the basement is already a game room with nothing anchoring it: pool table, brick fireplace, a twelve-foot blank wall and a landing with no purpose. A bar is the one piece of furniture that makes that wall and that floor make sense, and a bar beside a pool table gets used in a way a bar in a front room never does. Confidence: moderate.

## Where it goes

The blank wall at the foot of the stairs is the back-bar wall: long, unbroken, under the dropped soffit with two recessed cans already in it. The bar sits parallel to it, 42" out, so one person works behind it with the fridge door open. Stools on the open side; seated guests face the lit shelves, the bartender faces the room and the pool table. The pool table keeps 58" of cue clearance on every side and the bar and stools stay outside that line.

Two basement facts shape the design. The ceiling is low (roughly 7.5 ft under the soffit by the look of it), so the back bar stays horizontal: low cabinet and two long shelves, no tall hutch. Basements hold humidity, so slats are poplar not MDF, the plinth gets adjustable leveling feet, and nothing sits directly on the vinyl plank.

Depth used from the blank wall: 18 back bar + 42 aisle + 25 bar + 30 seated guest = 115". Tape it on the floor, including the cue clearance, before cutting anything. Wall length, ceiling height and the wall-to-rail distance are all unmeasured.

## The bar

Ergonomics (standard, high confidence)
- Finished height 42"
- Top 25" deep, 11" overhang on the guest side for knees, 14" body
- Foot rail centerline 7" above floor, 7" out from the face
- Stools 30" seat height, 24" centers, 3 stools on a 72" bar
- Plinth recessed 3" all round, 3" tall, LED strip underneath so the body floats
- Slats 1.5" wide on a 0.75" gap (2.25" pitch): 32 slats across the 72" front, 6 per end
- Shelves inside at 12" and 24": a 13" bottle bay and a glass bay, open to the bartender, no back panel

Body: two sheets of 3/4" plywood, pocket-screwed and glued. Front panel one piece (72 x 37.5) so the slat field has no seam. Painted inside and out in the console black.

Slat face: 1.5 x 0.75 strips, glued and 18-ga pinned to the painted front and ends. In a basement use 1x2 poplar (22 sticks of 8 ft, two slats each). MDF swells with humidity and telegraphs edges through paint; rip MDF only if the basement is dehumidified year-round under 50% RH.

Basement specifics: six 3/8" adjustable leveling feet on the plinth (slabs and vinyl plank are never flat), plinth held 3/4" off the floor so air moves under it, every ply edge sealed, LED driver inside the body not on the floor. Budget a dehumidifier if there is not one; it protects the acacia and the pool table felt alike.

Top: stock acacia butcher block 74 x 25 x 1.5. Sand to 180, dark walnut oil stain to pull it toward the dining table, then hardwax oil or Waterlox for spill resistance. Fix from below with figure-8 fasteners.

Rail, light, hardware: 1" black iron pipe foot rail (72" + two 90° elbows + two flanges), degreased and clear-coated matte. One 2700K COB LED strip under the plinth on a dimmer. Brass only on the back bar and alcove pulls, matched to the media console.

## Build sequence (three weekends)

1. Measure and tape the bar, back bar and the 58" cue clearance on the basement floor. Confirm the stools clear the cue line. Sit a chair where stools go. Walk the aisle with a fridge door open. Adjust length and aisle now.
2. Break down sheets, cut poplar slats to 37.5", paint slats black on sawhorses first.
3. Build the carcass: ends and divider to bottom, stretchers, square by diagonals, then the front panel. Build the 2x4 plinth separately and attach from inside with 3" screws, leaving a wire path.
4. Prime and paint the box inside and out. The front face must be fully black before slats go on; the gaps show it.
5. Hang slats with a 0.75" spacer block, full slat at every corner, glue and pin. Fill pin holes black.
6. Sand, stain, oil the top. Set on stretchers with figure-8s, 11" overhang to the guest side.
7. Degrease and clear-coat the pipe, assemble, flanges to plinth at 7" centerline. LED strip on the plinth face pointing down, driver inside the body.
8. Back bar: paint the wall to the soffit, find studs or furring strips, hang shelves, mount guitar hangers, assemble cabinet, slide in fridge, put the existing recessed cans on a dimmer.
9. Alcove last, with the leftovers, so every finish is from the same can.

Where to cut if you must
- Skip the back-bar cabinet, shelves only. Saves $180 to $280. Fridge goes inside the bar body.
- Rip slats from MDF instead of poplar. Saves about $100, only if the basement is dehumidified year-round. Otherwise a false saving.
- 2x12 glue-up top instead of acacia, stained to match. Saves $150, looks 80% as good, and your dining table will make the difference obvious.
- Alcove alone first: $350 to $500 with the second acacia block, one weekend.
