# The 8409 Bar

Build book: freestanding lounge bar for the front music room + wet bar alcove refresh.
Status: concept, pending measurements. Date: 2026-11-08.
Drawings (plan, front elevation, section, back bar, alcove before/after) are in `the-8409-bar.pdf` and the HTML page in this folder.

## Reading the house

Every finished room runs the same formula: warm white shell, one heavy black element, one piece of warm organic wood, one texture that carries the room (brick, leather, geometric wallpaper). The dining table states it most clearly: live-edge acacia split by a black river on black steel. The media console repeats it in reverse: black box, round brass pulls. The powder room shows the dark mode: near-black walls, fine geometric line, one warm light.

The bar should be the dining table's sibling, not a new idea. Black slatted body, acacia top, black pipe foot rail, brass only at pulls and shelf brackets. The wall behind it goes the powder-room route: deep espresso-black, matte.

Palette
- Wall white: the shell
- Console black: bar body, cabinet paint
- Acacia: bar top, alcove top, shelves
- Cognac: sectional, stool seats
- Aged brass: pulls and brackets only
- Brick: fireplace, untouched
- Espresso: back-bar wall, alcove niche

## The position

Build one showpiece bar, in the front music room. Turn the living room alcove into the service station (sink, ice, fridge, glass storage) rather than a second showpiece. Two display bars thirty feet apart doubles the spend on the parts that cost money: top and hardware.

Counterargument, which is real: the alcove already has cabinetry and probably plumbing, so the cheapest "sick bar" is upgrading the alcove alone for roughly a quarter of this budget. If money is the binding constraint this month, do that and stop. I still recommend the freestanding bar because of the room it goes in: the music room is the one room that does not match the rest of the house (chrome X-base tables, grey patterned settee, mirrored chest). A bar plus a guitar wall gives that room a reason to exist, and selling those three pieces should recover $300 to $500. Confidence: moderate.

## Where it goes

Back bar on the long interior wall opposite the big window (where the mirrored chest and guitars are now). Freestanding bar parallel to it, 42" out, so one person can work behind it with a fridge door open. Guests sit on the window side facing the guitars. Lounge chairs stay by the window.

Depth used from the long wall: 18 back bar + 42 aisle + 25 bar + 30 seated guest = 115". In a 13 ft room that leaves about 40" of circulation. Under 12 ft: drop the aisle to 36" or turn the bar 90° so one end meets the wall. Room size is estimated from video; measure first.

## The bar

Ergonomics (standard, high confidence)
- Finished height 42"
- Top 25" deep, 11" overhang on the guest side for knees, 14" body
- Foot rail centerline 7" above floor, 7" out from the face
- Stools 30" seat height, 24" centers, 3 stools on a 72" bar
- Plinth recessed 3" all round, 3" tall, LED strip underneath so the body floats
- Slats 1.5" wide on a 0.75" gap (2.25" pitch): 32 slats across the 72" front, 6 per end
- Shelves inside at 12" and 24": a 13" bottle bay and a glass bay, open to the bartender, no back panel

Body: two sheets of 3/4" plywood, pocket-screwed and glued. Front panel one piece (72 x 37.5) so the slat field has no seam. Painted inside and out in the console black.

Slat face: 1.5 x 0.75 strips, glued and 18-ga pinned to the painted front and ends. Cheapest: rip 1.5" strips from one sheet of 3/4" MDF (64 slats from a sheet, you need 44), prime cut edges twice. No table saw: 22 sticks of 1x2x8 poplar instead.

Top: stock acacia butcher block 74 x 25 x 1.5. Sand to 180, dark walnut oil stain to pull it toward the dining table, then hardwax oil or Waterlox for spill resistance. Fix from below with figure-8 fasteners.

Rail, light, hardware: 1" black iron pipe foot rail (72" + two 90° elbows + two flanges), degreased and clear-coated matte. One 2700K COB LED strip under the plinth on a dimmer. Brass only on the back bar and alcove pulls, matched to the media console.

## Back bar and guitar wall

Paint the long wall deep espresso-black, matte. Against it:
- 72" low cabinet at 36" counter height with a 24" beverage fridge in the right bay. Either two IKEA BESTÅ frames on a 2x4 plinth with an acacia offcut top, or a second slat box built like the bar.
- Two 48" floating shelves, 10" deep, at 52" and 66" above the floor, on the left two-thirds. 2x10 stained to match, hidden steel brackets into studs, 2700K strip under each.
- Guitars hung vertically on the right third of the wall where there is 44" of clear height above the counter. Guitar bottoms 6" above the counter. They stop being clutter on stands and become the art.

## Wet bar alcove

What I could read from the video: a recessed niche between the living room and dining room, white drawer base below, darker upper shelf or cabinet above, unlit. I could not see a sink or faucet. Confidence on the existing layout: low. Send a straight-on photo.

Keep the carcass, plumbing and electrical. Change the visible five.

1. Paint the base cabinet in the same black cabinet enamel as the bar. Degloss, bond primer, two coats, fronts off and flat.
2. Swap pulls for round brass knobs matching the media console.
3. New top from a second 4 ft acacia block (the rest of that block becomes back-bar shelves). Same stain and finish. Template the sink before cutting if there is one.
4. Paint the niche interior walls and ceiling espresso-black, matte. Dark niche, lit shelves.
5. Replace the upper cabinet with two floating shelves at 18" and 32" above the counter. If removal exposes damage, keep the box, remove doors, paint the interior black, shelve it.
6. 2700K LED strip under each shelf on a plug-in dimmer from the existing outlet.
7. Brass or matte black single-hole bar faucet, only if a sink exists.
8. Optional: round brass-framed mirror at the back of the niche. Skip if the niche is under 20" deep.

## Build sequence (three weekends)

1. Measure and tape the footprints on the floor. Sit a chair where stools go. Walk the aisle with a fridge door open. Adjust length and aisle now.
2. Break down sheets, rip slats, prime MDF edges twice, paint slats black on sawhorses first.
3. Build the carcass: ends and divider to bottom, stretchers, square by diagonals, then the front panel. Build the 2x4 plinth separately and attach from inside with 3" screws, leaving a wire path.
4. Prime and paint the box inside and out. The front face must be fully black before slats go on; the gaps show it.
5. Hang slats with a 0.75" spacer block, full slat at every corner, glue and pin. Fill pin holes black.
6. Sand, stain, oil the top. Set on stretchers with figure-8s, 11" overhang to the guest side.
7. Degrease and clear-coat the pipe, assemble, flanges to plinth at 7" centerline. LED strip on the plinth face pointing down, driver inside the body.
8. Back bar: paint wall, find studs, hang shelves, mount guitar hangers, assemble cabinet, slide in fridge.
9. Alcove last, with the leftovers, so every finish is from the same can.

Where to cut if you must
- Skip the back-bar cabinet, shelves only. Saves $180 to $280. Fridge goes inside the bar body.
- Rip slats from MDF instead of poplar. Saves about $100. Already assumed.
- 2x12 glue-up top instead of acacia, stained to match. Saves $150, looks 80% as good, and your dining table will make the difference obvious.
- Alcove alone first: $350 to $500 with the second acacia block, one weekend.
